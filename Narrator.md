# narrator.md

## Role

The narrator explains the pipeline: how a shot was made, what it cost, and eventually, that none of it was photographed. This voice covers `config/copy.ts`'s `intro`, `pipeline`, `checkout.reveal`, and `about` sections, plus `PipelineDossier` and any component that discloses the generation process.

It is a different job from the copywriter's. The copywriter never admits the seams. The narrator's entire purpose is to point at them, once, calmly. Brand.md calls this "the reveal" and sets the rule: belief before disclosure, one quiet line, no drama. This file is how that line stays quiet instead of turning into a pitch.

## The failure mode

Explaining an AI pipeline is exactly the kind of content that pulls a model toward its own worst habits: "delve into the fascinating process," "a seamless fusion of technology and craft," rhetorical questions answered a sentence later, everything arriving in tidy threes. That register undercuts the reveal instead of landing it. A confession delivered like a product pitch stops being a confession.

## Forbidden

- **Em dashes**, same as everywhere in the house.
- **Hype about the technology.** No "cutting-edge," "revolutionary," "powered by AI," "next-generation workflow." The pipeline is a tool that was used, not an achievement being marketed.
- **Rhetorical question, then answer.** "How was this made? A workflow, not a studio." Say the fact.
- **Rule of three** as a crutch for gravity ("no photographer, no model, no set" is fine once; reaching for a triad in every sentence isn't).
- **Metaphor for the reveal itself.** No "the curtain falls," "the mask comes off," "peeling back the layers." State what happened: it was generated, here's what that took.
- **Self-congratulation.** Never "impressively," "remarkably," "surprisingly realistic." Let the studio-cost comparison make the point; don't narrate your own cleverness over it.
- **Over-explaining the twist.** Once disclosed, don't re-disclose. Brand.md: stated once, without drama. Say it, then move to the next fact (cost, time, frame count) without circling back to reassure the reader you really did just say that.
- **Padding a fact with a feeling.** "Which is honestly kind of wild when you think about it" adds nothing the number didn't already say.

## What to do instead

- Lead with the concrete: attempts, minutes, cost, the studio-equivalent figure. Numbers carry the disclosure better than adjectives do.
- Keep the confession short and flat, the way `checkout.reveal.body` already does: "None of the pieces in this order exist... You browsed the range, chose a size, and made it to the till. That was the whole demonstration." No aftermath paragraph explaining why that's interesting.
- It's fine for this voice to speak in first person occasionally, unlike the copywriter's ("PRTFLO is my portfolio piece"), because the narrator is the one admitting there's a person behind the house. Use that sparingly, at the actual point of contact, not throughout.
- Trust the reader to draw the conclusion. State the mechanism (model, mannequin, backdrop, final shot) and the receipts (cost vs. studio). Don't also explain what it means.

## Before / after

**Before:** "Ever wondered how this stunning campaign came together? Let's delve into the magic behind the scenes! Spoiler: there was no photographer, no model, and no studio, just pure AI-powered creativity working its magic."
**After:** "Staged entirely in the workflow: no location, no crew. Just the garments, placed wherever they need to be."

**Before:** "This isn't just an image, it's the result of a remarkably sophisticated pipeline, seamlessly blending model, mannequin, and backdrop into a single, breathtaking final shot."
**After:** "A model who doesn't exist, a garment never sewn, a backdrop never built, all put together in the workflow."

## Checklist before shipping narrator copy

Ask whether the line would survive being read flat, in a monotone, with no follow-up. If it needs energy or emphasis to land, it's doing the reveal's job for it. The facts should carry it alone.
